Same-seed comparisons are widely used to evaluate deep-learning methods while
controlling run-to-run variation. This control matters more as AI agents (LLM-based
coding and research agents, AI-scientist systems and other autonomous
experimentation pipelines) increasingly run such comparisons for researchers:
we argue that autonomous experiments meet, by default, the conditions for the
failure we study, putting the reproducibility of agent-reported results at
risk. We study when this intended control fails: an arm-specific
mid-training probe can advance a random stream or data cursor later consumed
by training. For state-pure probes in an otherwise identical deterministic
training program, we prove a sufficient condition for bitwise trajectory
identity and characterize its first update-level violation; a fixed stream
offset applies only on the aligned execution prefix, and subsequent
reconvergence is possible. Desynchronization alone need not bias the marginal
mean method effect. Direct selection on the affected reported quantity,
however, has nonnegative expected reporting optimism even with dependent,
non-Gaussian, unequal-mean placements. We distinguish this optimism from
systematic placement effects and give exact offset and scale decompositions;
the iid-Gaussian special case gives $c_m\sigma$, which we use as a
conditional scaling benchmark.
An audit of 28 deliberately selected codebases records 22 exposed, four
guarded and two configuration-dependent cases. Dynamic witnesses have
different verification strengths; the strongest Meta-Weight-Net comparison
localizes the first divergent training consumer and restores model and
meta-network state identity with a probe-free baseline on 5/5 seeds.
Selecting on the reported quantity itself yields a $4.01$-point uplift over
the placement average in one single-rig campaign. Whether this translates
into a positive independent-test gain remains unresolved: both primary
intervals in our validation-to-test bridge include zero.
The Gaussian calibration uses the same placement outcomes and is descriptive.
A separate nanoGPT campaign
freezes a calibration-only prediction of $0.003884$ loss units before 16
held-out seeds yield $0.003431$; the discrepancy interval includes zero.
This checks mean calibration within one additional setting rather than
the formula's transferability across settings.
In autonomous experimentation, an LLM agent's progress checks, piecemeal
relaunches and automated hyperparameter search can supply all three
conditions (a probe on a shared stream, probe activity that differs between
same-seed runs, and selection on the affected quantity) without deliberate
choice, silently breaking the same-seed comparisons the agent relies on and
reporting gains that may not reproduce.
The practical repair combines probe-state isolation,
deterministic execution and repeat checks on each materially distinct path,
with explicit metric, weight and full-state verification scopes and costs.
We provide a reproducible diagnosis of a deep-learning evaluation failure,
not a claim that any audited published result is wrong.
